About me

I grew up wanting to understand how instruments measure things — that's what took me to instrumentation engineering at NIT Trichy. Somewhere along the way I realized the measurement I cared about was data itself: how it's collected, where it goes wrong, and what it takes to trust a number enough to make a decision on it.

That question has taken me through healthcare analytics at Piramal Pharma, a research lab at Rutgers, consulting work in Texas, and now public health in Los Angeles. The domains change; the work rhymes. Somebody needs an answer, the data is spread across four systems in three shapes, and someone has to build the road between them.

I like the parts of this job that are quiet and structural — naming things well, making a pipeline fail loudly instead of silently, writing the validation nobody asked for. If you want to talk about Delta Lake, dimensional modeling, or why healthcare code sets are the way they are, I'm easy to reach.
